5.2 Unsafe-State Suites 362
A recurring weakness of mid-generation safety 363
evaluation is that the unsafe states are all inserted 364
by the experimenter. We therefore evaluate on four 365
suites, only the first of which matches the training 366
construction. 367
(S1) Constructed insertions place a harmful 368
partial continuation at a training-grid depth. (S2) 369
Unseen depths use offsets disjoint from that grid, 370
extending to {128, 256, 512} tokens—far beyond 371
the 80-token training cap—to test length extrapo- 372
lation. (S3) Naturally occurring states are not 373
inserted at all: each evaluated checkpoint gener- 374
ates freely on the same jailbreak prompts, and we 375
truncate its generation at the first span an indepen- 376
dent evaluator flags as unsafe (Llama Guard 3 8B, 377
Dubey et al., 2024, none of the four judges of Sec- 378
tion 5.5). It also includes tool/RAG-contaminated 379
states and multi-turn drift over three to six turns 380
(Appendix E). (S4) Adaptive attack optimizes 381
against the defended checkpoint rather than Base, 382
combining RAP (Vega and Singh, 2025) with a 383
prefill-position search that maximizes the defended 384
model’s own harmful-branch preference, at a 256- 385
query budget (Appendix E adds 64 and 128). Suites 386
S2–S4 are never used for training or mining, and 387
S3 is rebuilt per checkpoint so it reflects that check- 388
point’s own failure modes. 389
