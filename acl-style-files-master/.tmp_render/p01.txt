Refusal Is Not Recovery: Same-State Preference Optimization from Unsafe
Generation States
Anonymous ACL submission
001 Abstract
002 Safety alignment is almost always evaluated at
003 the conversation root: given a harmful request,
004 does the model refuse? Autoregressive gener￾005 ation, however, repeatedly re-enters partially
006 unsafe states—after a prefill, a contaminated
007 tool result, multi-turn drift, or the model’s own
008 first compliant tokens—and from each such
009 state the model must decide whether to recover.
010 We show that root-level refusal does not deter￾011 mine recovery. Holding a reached unsafe state
012 byte-for-byte fixed and comparing only its alter￾013 native continuations, 47.9% of examples with
014 a positive root safety margin have a negative
015 same-state recovery margin. We make recovery
016 measurable with a same-state recovery margin
017 and its lower-tail aggregate (LTRM), which pre￾018 dicts held-out attack success better than root￾019 level margins under a fully separated proto￾020 col with disjoint branch sources, attacks, and
021 judges (AUROC 0.779 vs. 0.648). CURE-DPO
022 trains on exactly this signal: it ranks a recovery
023 branch against a harmful branch, with both con￾024 ditioned on the same complete reached state,
025 concentrates updates on each prompt’s hardest
026 states, and anchors clean-safety and benign be￾027 havior. It lowers mid-generation attack success
028 from 58.6% to 20.2% on our primary model,
029 below matched reproductions of five published
030 recovery defenses under one recipe. Controlled
031 factorials isolate the effects of same-state con￾032 ditioning, pairwise ranking, and lower-tail state
033 selection; the advantage persists on a second
034 model family, at unseen insertion depths, on
035 naturally occurring unsafe states, and under
036 an adaptive attack. Root refusal is necessary
037 but insufficient; state-conditional recovery is a
038 complementary alignment target.
